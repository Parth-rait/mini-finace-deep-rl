About

Calmer decisions, one trade at a time.

Markets swing hardest when many people act on the same feeling at once: fear of missing out, panic, the need to win back a loss. This helps one person at a time notice that state, before and after a trade, using evidence rather than hindsight.

How it works

  1. Plan. You set an entry, a stop (where you'd accept you were wrong), an optional target and a time limit. Code works out what the stop costs, the reward to risk and the share of your account at risk. Your reason is read for mood and seven state signals (fear, greed, regret, frustration, certainty, urgency, herd talk), and for six behavioural biases, each quoting your words. A heat score combines them with fixed weights. A regime model (calm, normal or crisis), fitted on prices up to today, simulates 1,000 paths for the hold you plan: how often they touch your stop or reach your target, and how wide the outcomes spread.
  2. Review. The same model, fitted only on data up to your buy date, places your actual result among 1,000 paths from that day's regime. Trading days are counted on the exchange calendar.
  3. Journal. Plans and trades are kept under a private ID, frozen as planned. Each trade is checked against its plan on daily closes: on plan, stop crossed, target reached, time limit passed, and once closed, whether you followed the plan, broke it or sold early. Over time it shows how you do when you follow your plans, and when you plan calm or hot.

Your journal and your privacy

  • No account and no password. Your journal is reached with a private ID; anyone with the ID can see its trades, so keep it like a password.
  • Only what you enter is stored. You can delete a trade or the whole journal at any time.

Limits

  • Not a forecast and not advice. The regime model has no predictive edge; it measures luck, not skill.
  • Single-factor model that understates tail risk. Daily closes, no slippage, no borrow costs on shorts.
  • Not covered: crypto, individual bonds, non-US listings. Options are reviewed as the underlying stock.
  • The readers are tested on small or noisy labels (E08, E09). Treat them as prompts to reflect, not verdicts.
