Calmer decisions, one trade at a time

Markets swing hardest when many people act on the same feeling at once: fear of missing out, panic, the need to win back a loss. This project helps one person at a time notice that state before and after a trade, using the evidence rather than hindsight.

How a review works

  1. You pick the trade. The stock comes from the daily list of every US-listed stock and ETF, the dates from a calendar. Trading days between them are counted by code on the exchange calendar: a buy on a holiday moves to the next session, a sell on a weekend uses the Friday close. If you describe the trade in words instead, a language model fills the form and you check it.
  2. Check the data. Daily prices come from Yahoo Finance through a validated cache. Unknown or delisted symbols, renamed tickers, listings that started after your buy date, too little history and future dates are explained rather than worked around.
  3. Luck check. A regime model (calm, normal or crisis) is fitted on market data up to your buy date only and simulates 1,000 paths from that day's regime. Your result is placed among them. The 3D view shows those outcomes for every day you could have held, with your actual path drawn across.
  4. Bias signals. Your stated reasoning is checked for six behavioural biases from the finance literature: FOMO, herding, overconfidence, anchoring, loss aversion and revenge trading. Every signal must quote your own words.
  5. Context and explanation. What the S&P 500 did over the same days, and a short summary in which every number is filled in by code from the computed results.

Your journal and your privacy

  • No account and no password. Your journal is reached with a private ID; anyone who has the ID can see its trades, so keep it like a password.
  • Only what you enter is stored: the trade, your reasons, and its review. You can delete a trade or the whole journal at any time.

Limits

  • The luck check measures where an outcome sits within a model's range of luck. It is not a forecast and not a measure of skill.
  • The regime model is single-factor and understates tail risk. Daily closes, no slippage, no borrow costs on shorts.
  • Not covered: crypto, individual bonds, non-US listings. Options are reviewed as the underlying stock.
  • The bias classifier agrees moderately with human labels (Cohen's kappa 0.42 on 50 posts) and misreads sarcasm.
  • Nothing here is investment advice, and this site never places orders.
