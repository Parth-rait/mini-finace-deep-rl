    How a review works

    1. You pick the trade. The stock comes from the daily list of every US-listed stock and ETF (NASDAQ, NYSE, NYSE Arca, Cboe). The dates come from a calendar. Trading days between them are counted by code on the exchange calendar: a buy on a holiday moves to the next session, a sell on a weekend uses the Friday close. If you describe the trade in words instead, a language model fills the form and you check it; a date that could be read two ways, or a day the text never gave, is left for you to pick.
    2. Check the data. Daily prices come from Yahoo Finance through a validated cache. Unknown or delisted symbols, renamed tickers, listings that started after your buy date, too little history, and future dates are explained rather than worked around.
    3. Luck check. A regime model (calm, normal or crisis) is fitted on market data up to your buy date only and simulates 1,000 paths from that day's regime. Your result is placed among them. The 3D view shows those outcomes for every day you could have held, with your actual path drawn across.
    4. Bias signals. Your stated reasoning is checked for six behavioural biases from the finance literature: FOMO, herding, overconfidence, anchoring, loss aversion and revenge trading. Every signal must quote your own words.
    5. Context. What the S&P 500 and the research stocks did over the same days.
    6. Explanation. A language model writes a short summary with placeholders where numbers go; the code fills them in from the computed results, and any number it can't account for sends the summary back to a plain template.

    Not covered

    • Crypto, individual bonds and non-US listings. Bond ETFs (TLT, IEF, SHY, AGG) and US-listed ADRs work.
    • Options are reviewed as the underlying stock, with a note: leverage, strike and time decay are not modelled.
    • Holds longer than 252 trading days (about a year), and trades opened and closed on the same day.

    Limits

    • The luck check measures where an outcome sits within a model's range of luck. It is not a forecast and not a measure of skill.
    • The regime model is single-factor and understates tail risk. Daily closes, no slippage.
    • The bias classifier agrees moderately with human labels (Cohen's kappa 0.42 on 50 posts) and misreads sarcasm.
    • Nothing here is investment advice, and this site never places orders.
